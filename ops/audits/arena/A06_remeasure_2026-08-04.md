# A06 re-measured — against the Design Template this time

**Card:** A06, re-run. Read-only: no CSS, JS, template, database or production
change. Owner-assigned to Bolt 2026-08-04 after the original matrix was found to
have measured a rejected source.

## Why this exists

`A06_measurement_matrix_2026-07-29.md` states its reference source in its own
lines 6–8: `ops/prototypes/arena_visual_shell/`. The Owner had **rejected** that
prototype. Its headline figure — widen and flatten the octagon from aspect 1.30
to **2.05** — was handed straight to card A07 and was one instruction away from
being built.

Nothing in that matrix was edited. Re-measuring is not the same job as
overwriting, and the old figures are evidence of how a wrong source produces
confident numbers.

## The number that was holding A07

```text
Design Template floor grid:  1585.4 × 667.5   aspect 2.375
Rejected prototype (A06):     740   × 363     aspect 2.05
```

**The reference aspect is 2.375, not 2.05.** Measured three ways in the same
session and identical each time: the 52-child grid element, and its two parent
wrappers at 1617.6 × 678.9 (2.382) and 1675 × 699 (2.396). The floor is centred
at x = 960 on a 1920 canvas, dead centre.

So the *direction* in the old matrix survives — production's octagon is too
narrow and too round, and must become wider and shallower — but the **target was
wrong by 0.325 of aspect**, about 16%. An octagon built to 2.05 would have been
visibly rounder than the approved design, and the error would have looked like
the builder's.

### Three things this column settles

**1. The octagon aspect is confirmed independently.** The old matrix reported
production at ≈1.30 measured on 2026-07-29; this run measures **1.266** by a
different route on a different day. Production was never the disputed half of
that matrix — only the reference was — and this agrees with it. So the shape
delta is real and always was; only its target moved.

**2. The fighter composition is genuinely absent, and that is not a reference
dispute.** The reference places four plinth blocks in two symmetric pairs
flanking the crown. Production has one chef avatar, 66.5 × 69.9, sitting at
cx 831 while the octagon centres at 955 — asymmetric, small, and anchored to
whichever cell the chef happens to occupy. This is the Owner's "dead stickers"
report, measured.

**3. The phase panel is at parity and A11 is why.** 271.6 × 119.5 against the
reference's 278 × 124 is within five pixels on both axes. Before A11 the panel
carried the page-refresh countdown in the reference's clock position; the size
was never the problem there, and the numbers now say so.

## What this changes for the queue

- **A07 is unblocked and has both halves.** Target aspect **2.375**, not 2.05.
  Concretely, at 1920: the floor must go from 966.9 × 763.7 to 1585.4 × 667.5 —
  **about +64% wider and −13% shorter** — while staying centred, and the octagon
  currently centres at 955 against the reference's 960. Building to 2.05 is now
  a known error rather than an open question.
- **A09** (fighter composition) gains real numbers: two symmetric plinth pairs
  flanking the crown at y ≈ 649–665, centres at x 557/1363 and 619/1301 on a
  1920 canvas. The original matrix's finding — that production has no fighter
  composition at all, only scattered cell avatars — is about production and is
  unaffected by the reference dispute.
- **A11** shipped against this same reference and the numbers agree with it, but
  only once two different elements stop being confused for each other:
  - the **phase card** — the part A11 rebuilt — is 271.6 × 119.5 against the
    reference's 278 × 124. Parity, within five pixels on both axes.
  - the **whole top-left block** that contains it is 304 × 253.8 against the
    reference's 316 × 227, so it is still **26.8 too tall**. That is the
    remaining gap, and it belongs to whatever card frames the panel, not to A11.

  The 245.6-and-245.1 figures quoted in the A11 release notes were the top-left
  block measured at **1280**, not the phase card at 1920. They were correct for
  what they measured and would be wrong to read as a comparison against the
  reference, which declares its canvas at 1920.

## Reproducing this

```bash
cd ops/reference/design_arena
python -m http.server 8795 --bind 127.0.0.1
# open http://127.0.0.1:8795/Chef%20Battles%20Arena%20v2.dc.html at 1920x1080
# floor = the div with exactly 52 children and width > 800
```

Do not remove the `.gitattributes` in that directory. Without its `-text` the
HTML and JS get CRLF on a Windows checkout, every byte offset moves, and the
file measured stops being the file that was exported.

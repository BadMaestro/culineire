# A06 — re-measured against the Design Template, and corrected

**Card:** A06, re-run. Read-only: no CSS, JS, template, database or production
behaviour changed. Owner-assigned to Bolt 2026-08-04.

**Status:** complete against the card's acceptance except one row, named at the
end. This supersedes the first version of itself, which reached a wrong

---

## 1. The correction, first

The first version of this re-measure told card A07 to make the octagon **"about
64 percent wider and 13 percent shorter."** That instruction was wrong, and it
sat on the live build board for several hours.

**Wrong comparison.** It set the Design Template's whole floor square against
production's **rank cells only**: 1585.4 wide against 966.9. Those are not the
same object. Like for like the aspects are **1.467 against 2.375**, not 1.266
against 2.375.

**Wrong cause, which is worse.** GreenBear established in v2.5.792 that the
template's floor is a **1120 × 1120 SQUARE** with no scaling in its transform
matrix. Its 2.375 comes entirely from its **camera** — `rotateX(57deg)`,
`perspective: 1600px`. Applied to our own SVG on the live page, that camera
projects our square to 1585.4 × 667.5: the reference floor to the decimal.

So the octagon was never the wrong shape. The difference was the camera. An
agent following my instruction would have stretched correct geometry to imitate
a projection, and it would have looked like their mistake.

**Where the camera stands.** v2.5.792 shipped 57 degrees; the Owner looked at it
on production and said put 42 back. v2.5.793 reverted it whole. `rotateX(42deg)`
is the standing camera and this document does not reopen it.

What survives from the first version, unchanged and independently reproduced:
the 2026-07-29 matrix measured `ops/prototypes/arena_visual_shell/`, which the
Owner had rejected, and its 2.05 came from there.

## 6. What the matrix actually says

**The fighter composition is the one large, uncontested gap.** The reference
places **two portrait blocks, 190 × 212, centred at x 700 and x 1220** — exactly
260 px either side of the canvas centre and symmetric to the pixel. Production
draws its floor fighters only while the centre is an `active_battle` or a
`facing_pair`; with a crown in the centre, as on both measurement days, there
are none, and what this document originally measured as "fighters" was one
66.4 × 69.8 cell avatar — a chef seated in a rank ring, not a fighter. This is
the Owner's "dead stickers" report, measured. It belongs to A09.

## 6a. Correction, 2026-08-05: the "plinths" were floor cells

The two rows struck out in §5 were wrong, and the error is the useful part.

They were read as four plinth blocks in two symmetric pairs flanking the crown,
and card A09 carried that reading. **They are cells of the reference's own
52-cell floor grid** — the near rows, where its 57° camera makes them large.
Verified rather than argued: each block was matched by size and centre against
the children of the element that has exactly 52 of them, and both matched. The
two `image-slot` elements that ARE the fighters sit outside that grid.

So the fighter target is one block per side, not two, and it is 190 × 212 at an
offset of 260 px — not 196 × 244 at 402.8 and 341.3.

**Only the x transfers.** The 260 px offset is camera-free, because `rotateX`
leaves x untouched. The y does not: the portrait is 212 tall against 190 wide
and stands upright above the crown, while this project's floor fighters lie flat
on the floor plane by deliberate choice (a nested 3D billboard shattered the
camera, v2.5.649). Converting that y into a floor offset through the camera is
the same mistake this document was written to correct in the first place.

**The panels are close; the ladder is the outlier.** Phase panel 271.6 × 119.5
against 278 × 124 is parity within five pixels. Crown ladder 248 × 166.6 against
316 × 272 is the largest furniture gap on the page.

**The octagon delta is a camera question, not a geometry one**, and the camera
is settled at 42 degrees by the Owner. This document therefore hands A07 **no
target aspect at all**: the previous one was wrong, and inventing a replacement
would repeat exactly the mistake being corrected.

## 8. On screenshots, and why none are stored here

**There is no open row. The Owner settled it on 2026-08-04:**

> a screenshot is used once, to identify a problem; after that it is physically
> unnecessary and is classified as junk to be deleted completely.

That resolves what the earlier draft of this section called a blocker. It was
not a blocker, it was a wrong premise: I had read "side-by-side images" as
"files committed to the repository" and then reported a tool limit for not
producing them. The tool limit is real and irrelevant.

What acceptance needs is the **comparison**, and the comparison is in the
numbers. So the artefacts kept here are the ones that stay true and stay
readable:

- the **JSON measurements**, which are the evidence itself — diffable,
  re-runnable, and the thing any later disagreement is settled against;
- the **side-by-side SVGs**, generated from those numbers, both panes at one
  scale, each box labelled with its real pixel size. A few kilobytes of text
  that says exactly what it knows and nothing it does not;
- the **method and the reproduce command**, so anyone can regenerate the
  picture in a minute instead of trusting a stored one.

The screenshots used to reach these numbers were exactly that: used, then
deleted. Fourteen stored diagnostic screenshots elsewhere in `ops/audits/` went
with them in the same release — 6.87 MB of pictures of pages that have since
changed, which is the other half of why storing them is a bad idea. A stored
screenshot ages into a confident lie: it keeps looking authoritative long after
the page stopped looking like that.

What was NOT deleted, because none of it is a diagnostic screenshot: the paid
crowd contact sheets, the mockup crops, the hall wash asset, the reference's own
`greenbear.png`, and the approved mockup itself.

## 9. Reproducing this

```bash
cd ops/reference/design_arena
python -m http.server 8795 --bind 127.0.0.1
# open http://127.0.0.1:8795/Chef%20Battles%20Arena%20v2.dc.html
# floor = the div with exactly 52 children and width > 500
# production floor = union bbox of .arena-cell[data-ring-kind="rank"], 184 cells
```

Do not remove the `.gitattributes` in the reference directory. Without its
`-text` the HTML and JS get CRLF on a Windows checkout, every byte offset moves,
and the file measured stops being the file that was exported.
